Public health2016–2022

National Integrated Health Data System

A real-time disease surveillance reporting backbone for Nigerian health facilities, adopted into federal and state operations.

Schematic. System architecture and the reporting gaps it exposed. Facility-level surveillance figures are government data and are not reproduced here.

Context

Notifiable disease reports moved on paper from facility to local government to state to the national desk. By the time a signal arrived it was weeks old, and nobody could distinguish a genuinely quiet state from one that had simply stopped reporting.

Approach

I designed and deployed a system that captured facility returns electronically, reconciled every record against the national master facility list, and computed completeness and timeliness by state and facility tier. Threshold rules were written to match IDSR case definitions rather than invented locally, so a flag meant the same thing everywhere.

What it showed

  • Silence is not absence. Several low-count states were not low-incidence; they had stopped submitting. Separating non-reporting from zero-reporting changed the national picture.
  • Latency was concentrated. Most of the delay sat in one or two handoffs, not spread evenly across the chain, which meant a targeted fix was possible.
  • Identity was the bottleneck. Until facilities were reconciled to a single register, no aggregate could be trusted.

Impact

Adopted into federal and state operations. The definitions and workflow it enforced fed into the Federal Ministry of Health's 2019 Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response guidelines. Manual reporting effort fell by roughly 70%.

Public health2016

National Health Facility Survey digitisation

Turned Nigeria's 2016 facility census from paper instruments into the structured master register every downstream system validated against.

Schematic. The reduction sequence from raw survey instruments to a single authoritative register. Record counts are government data and are not published here.

Context

National health statistics were being published against a facility list that existed largely on paper and in local variants. Two agencies counting clinics in the same district could produce different totals, and both could defend their number.

Approach

I led digitisation of the survey instruments, designed the coding scheme and the validation rules that rejected impossible records at entry, resolved duplicate and near-duplicate facility entries, and built the reconciliation process for ongoing updates.

What it showed

  • Duplication was systematic, not random. The same facility appeared under abbreviated, renamed, and relocated forms, which meant name matching alone was never going to be enough.
  • Most rejected records failed on a handful of rules, so fixing the collection instrument was cheaper than cleaning downstream.

Impact

A single authoritative register that the Integrated Health Data System and national reporting validated against. It removed an entire category of argument from the reporting cycle.

Clinical AI · in development2026

BridgePath — psychiatric readmission risk

A discharge-time tool that sorts adult psychiatric inpatients into transitional-support tiers by 30-day readmission risk. Specification complete, awaiting a data sponsor.

Schematic — design specification, not results. No patient data has been accessed. Tier labels and the monitoring loop are from the drafted product definition.

Context

Working an inpatient psychiatric unit, I kept seeing the same people return inside a month. Discharge planning capacity is finite and allocated largely by clinician memory, so the patients who most need transitional support are not reliably the ones who get it.

Approach

I wrote the full product definition: risk tiers assigned at discharge, social workers and discharge planners as the daily users, and a monitoring dashboard tracking calibration and subgroup performance after deployment. The tool recommends supports only and never rules on discharge readiness. That boundary is designed in, not disclaimed in a footer.

Design decisions worth naming

  • Calibration over accuracy. A tiering tool is useless if a "high risk" label does not correspond to an actual probability, so calibration is the primary metric.
  • Subgroup monitoring from day one, because a model that performs well overall can fail badly on a specific population.
  • Override rate is tracked as a signal, not suppressed. If clinicians routinely disagree with the tool, the tool is wrong.

Status

Specification and monitoring plan drafted. A hospital sponsor and governance approval precede any model training, and identifiers would remain on hospital infrastructure.

Clinical AI · proposal2026

EEG seizure detection support

An internal proposal for machine-assisted seizure detection on EEG, written for hospital management rather than for engineers.

Schematic. Illustrative EEG trace and the proposed screen-then-review workflow. No patient recordings are shown.

Context

EEG review is time-intensive and event-sparse. Long recordings with rare events are exactly the setting where automated screening helps most, and exactly where a badly specified model does the most damage.

Approach

I built the case for a detection-support workflow and wrote it for a management audience: what would be automated, what stays with the technologist, how false negatives would be caught, and what would have to be true before anyone relied on it.

The argument

  • Screening, not diagnosis. The model reorders the review queue; it never issues a finding.
  • False negatives are the real risk. A sampled re-read audit is the only way to measure them, so it is part of the proposal rather than a later addition.
  • Adoption is a workflow question. If flagged segments do not land where technologists already work, the tool goes unused regardless of accuracy.

Status

Delivered as a written proposal and a management presentation. Included here because framing a clinical AI case for non-technical decision-makers is much of what a hospital analyst actually does.

Operations analytics2026

Shift coverage automation

An automated staff-scheduling workflow for a hospital duty floor: decline and opt-in handling, rotation-based auto-matching, response windows, and an admin dashboard.

Schematic. Workflow states and dashboard panels from the drafted requirements document. No staffing figures are shown.

Context

Call-offs and no-shows were absorbed manually: a supervisor phoning down a list under time pressure, with no record of who had already been asked, who had declined, or how extra shifts were distributed across staff.

Approach

I designed the workflow end to end. Eligible staff are alerted in rotation order rather than first-come, each alert carries a timed response window, and an expired window passes automatically to the next person. Attendance and burden are logged, and an admin dashboard shows coverage gaps before they become emergencies.

Why it is an analytics problem

  • The core is allocation and fairness, not a calendar. Who gets asked, in what order, and how often, is a measurable distribution.
  • Response behaviour is data. Knowing who answers at 2am and who never answers changes the order the system should use.
  • The manual process left no trace, which meant no one could show whether the burden was shared evenly.

Status

Product requirements document drafted, built on the existing manual scheduling process rather than replacing it wholesale.

Genomics2023

RNA-seq differential expression pipeline

An end-to-end, re-runnable pipeline from raw reads to a ranked, annotated gene list, built so the result reproduces from a single script.

Schematic. Pipeline architecture with its QC gates, and an illustrative volcano showing how the output separates. Point positions are drawn for explanation, not from a specific dataset.

Context

Differential expression results are easy to produce and easy to get wrong. Most of the damage happens before modelling: unbalanced libraries, adapter contamination, a sample that quietly failed and got patched instead of dropped.

Approach

I built the full path from raw reads to results, then wired the diagnostics in as gates rather than afterthoughts. Read-level quality control, adapter and quality trimming, alignment to a reference, gene-level counting, normalisation, and differential expression with dispersion estimation and false-discovery-rate control. If a sample fails a gate the run stops instead of continuing quietly.

What matters in the output

  • Effect size and significance are different questions. The volcano exists so neither gets read alone.
  • Multiple testing is the whole game. Thousands of genes tested at once means an uncorrected p-value is close to meaningless.
  • Sample structure is checked before testing. If principal components separate by batch rather than condition, the result is about the batch.

Impact

Presented as Identification of Differentially Expressed Genes of RNA-seq with Computational Pipeline Tools (Ajiboye & Chin, 2023). It became the template I reuse for any counting-based expression question.

Genomics · MSc. thesis2024

Spatially variable gene identification with BSP

A dimension-agnostic, granularity-based method for finding genes whose expression varies across tissue space, benchmarked across platforms.

Schematic. How the two-scale comparison distinguishes genuine spatial variation from an artefact of sampling granularity. Tissue patterns are illustrative.

Context

Spatial transcriptomics platforms disagree on resolution, dimensionality, and density. A method tuned to one platform's granularity loses signal on another, which makes results hard to compare across studies.

Approach

I applied and evaluated big-small patch analysis for spatially variable gene identification, structured deliberately to be agnostic to dimension and to sampling granularity, then benchmarked its behaviour across datasets from different platforms.

What the benchmarking showed

  • Granularity is a confound, not a setting. A gene that only looks variable at one patch size is usually telling you about the sampling, not the tissue.
  • Comparability requires a scale-free criterion, otherwise every cross-study comparison inherits the platform's resolution.

Impact

Master's thesis in Bioinformatics, Indiana University Indianapolis, advised by Jingwen Yan and Juexin Wang. The benchmarking discipline transfers directly to model selection anywhere: compare like for like, and state where a method stops working.

Alzheimer's disease progression modelling

Contributed to model development for disease progression, using latent-variable approaches that capture trajectories rather than single time points.

Schematic. The modelling problem in one picture: cross-sectional scores can coincide while trajectories diverge. Curves are illustrative, not patient data.

Context

Alzheimer's is a trajectory, not a state. Cross-sectional comparison flattens it, and patients arrive at any point along the curve, so two people with identical scores today can be heading in very different directions.

Approach

I worked within the research group on model development, handling data preparation, feature construction from genetic and imaging-derived measures, and evaluation.

What the approach buys

  • Latent variables absorb the unobserved. Disease stage is not measured directly, so the model has to estimate where a patient sits on the curve rather than assume everyone starts together.
  • Calibration matters more than accuracy when the output is a risk over time rather than a label.

Drug discovery2024

Sickle cell therapeutic target discovery

A computational screen for novel targets and pathways, narrowing the candidate space before anyone spends bench time on it.

Schematic. The filtering logic, each stage narrowing on a different criterion. Bar widths express the shape of the reduction, not counts.

Context

Sickle cell disease is common, under-funded relative to its burden, and its discovery pipeline is thin. Wet-lab screening is the bottleneck, so anything that ranks candidates credibly beforehand buys real time.

Approach

I ran a computational analysis identifying candidate therapeutic targets and the pathways they sit in, then prioritised by pathway relevance and druggability rather than single-gene signal alone.

Why the ordering matters

  • Statistical significance is the weakest filter. It comes first because it is cheap, not because it is the most informative.
  • Pathway context rescues and rejects. A modest signal inside a coherent mechanism can outrank a strong isolated one.
  • Druggability is a hard constraint. A perfect target nobody can bind to is not a target.

Impact

Presented with S. Biwalkar and A. Isla as Identifying Novel Therapeutic Targets and Pathways for Sickle Cell Disease. Runner-up, 3 Minute Thesis, IUPUI Preparing Future Faculty and Professionals conference, 2023.

Genomics · published2022

Genomic signature prediction and drug-likeness

Predicting genomic signatures of sequencing reads and comparing drug-likeness across candidate compounds. Master's thesis, later published.

Schematic. The feature-construction chain from raw reads through classification to compound screening. Bars and points are illustrative.

Context

Sequence composition carries signal that can classify and characterise organisms, and that signal can be connected to screening for compound properties. It only works if the feature construction is disciplined enough to generalise beyond the training set.

Approach

I built genomic signature features from NGS sequence data, trained classification models on them, and ran comparative drug-likeness assessment across the candidate set.

What made it work

  • No assembly required. Composition-based features are computable directly from reads, which removes the most expensive and error-prone step.
  • Drug-likeness is a set of coarse filters, not a prediction of efficacy, and the write-up says so rather than overselling it.

Impact

Master's thesis in Functional Mathematics, Imo State University, supervised by Dr G.A. Okeke. Published as Okeke & G.A in the International Journal of Research Publication and Reviews.

Financial analytics2026

Starbucks three-year performance analysis

Ratio, trend, and forecasting analysis built entirely from filed statements, with every figure traceable to a 10-K line item.

Real figures. Taken from Starbucks Form 10-K filings for FY2023–FY2025 and the Q4 FY2025 earnings release, via SEC EDGAR. FY2024 is omitted from the chart for legibility; it is covered in the full analysis.

Context

Starbucks' operating margin fell from 16.3% to 7.9% across three years while revenue kept climbing from roughly $36.0B to $37.2B. The question was whether the decline was cyclical or structural, and what it implied for how the company should fund itself.

Approach

I pulled FY2023–FY2025 Form 10-K filings and the Q4 FY2025 earnings release from SEC EDGAR, normalised line items across the three years, and computed liquidity, margin, and leverage ratios. I benchmarked operating margin against McDonald's and ran percent-of-sales forecasting.

What the numbers showed

  • Revenue growth masked margin collapse. Net income more than halved, from $4.13B to $1.86B, while the top line rose. A revenue-only reading would have missed the entire story.
  • Liquidity was already tight and got tighter. The current ratio slid from 0.78 to 0.72, and total liabilities stayed near 125% of assets, leaving a persistent shareholders' equity deficit.
  • Cash generation weakened alongside earnings. Free cash flow fell from $3.68B to $2.44B, which constrains the buyback and dividend programme the equity deficit depends on.
  • Percent-of-sales forecasting breaks here. The method assumes cost ratios hold, and the whole finding is that they did not, so I stated that limitation in the recommendation instead of burying it.

Impact

Delivered as a written analysis and a nineteen-slide deck with a financing recommendation and an original financing-flow diagram mapping each funding source to its use.

Research design2026

Consumer trust in wearable health alerts

A quantitative study design on hypertension notifications from consumer smartwatches: does perceived accuracy drive trust, and does privacy concern cut against it?

Study design. The hypothesised relationships and how they would be tested. No data has been collected; this is the analysis plan.

Context

Consumer wearables now surface clinically meaningful alerts to people with no clinician in the loop. Whether those alerts change behaviour depends less on sensor accuracy than on whether the wearer trusts them, and trust is not the same variable as accuracy.

Approach

I designed a quantitative study with a stratified sampling frame and a hypothesis chain running from perceived accuracy through trust to behavioural intent, with privacy concern modelled as a competing variable rather than a control. The design is grounded in current literature on wearable hypertension notification.

Design choices

  • Trust is a mediator, not a proxy. Treating it as a mediator lets the study separate "the watch is right" from "I will act on it".
  • Privacy concern gets its own path because it can suppress action even when trust is high.
  • Stratified sampling because adoption of wearables is uneven across age and income, and a convenience sample would bake that in.

Impact

A complete research plan and presentation. It is the methodological counterpart to the modelling work: deciding what to measure and how to sample before touching an analysis.

Social enterprise · founder2023–

FoodClick

A platform converting verified educational engagement into food access credits, so learning activity becomes a measurable household benefit.

Schematic. The engagement-to-access loop and the measurement points that make the link auditable rather than merely plausible.

Context

Food assistance and educational engagement are usually run as separate programmes with separate funding and separate measurement, so neither gets credit for reinforcing the other.

Approach

I designed a model where verified educational engagement earns food access credits, together with the metrics that would make the link defensible to a funder.

The hard part was definition

  • What counts as engagement? Enrolment is easy to record and means nothing. Completion is harder to verify and means something.
  • How do you stop it being gamed? Speed-runs and duplicate accounts are the obvious attacks, so the metrics include detection for both.
  • What would falsify the model? If redemption does not lead to repeat engagement, the loop is not closing and the premise fails.

Impact

EdTech · founder2025–

TRI.IO Bootcamp

A four-week children's coding programme with age-banded tracks running from block coding to JavaScript, SQL, APIs, and responsible AI use, ending in a demo day.

Programme design. Track progression across the four weeks. Shading indicates increasing complexity, not assessment scores.

Context

Most children's coding programmes stop at puzzles. Very few take an eleven-year-old as far as querying a database or consuming a live API, and almost none teach AI tools with any ethical framing.

Approach

I built four progressive tracks with session-by-session curricula, defined learning outcomes per track, capped classes at twelve with one instructor plus an assistant, and modelled the operating and revenue picture for each cohort.

Design choices

  • Every track ends in something shippable. A demo day and a portfolio artefact, because a certificate alone teaches nothing about finishing.
  • AI tooling is taught with its ethics attached, including bias in algorithms, rather than as a productivity trick.
  • Peer code review is on the timetable. Giving and receiving feedback is the professional skill most programmes skip.

Status
